Adjust: the frame, the yard lines, the play window, the snap and the offense

1Pick a still pre-snap frame

The frame the field is calibrated on. The suggested one is the last still frame before the play moves. Motion is the bar under each frame.

2Name the yard lines

Pick any line you can read a number on — the one through a painted 10, 20, 30... is easiest. The others follow 5 yards apart; a line the spacing says is not a yard line (the edge of end-zone paint, an overlay) is marked ✕ and left out.

Lines missed or wrong?

The yellow grid should sit on the painted yard lines, hashes and sidelines wherever players stand, and its 10s on the lines through painted numbers. Magenta circles are the landmarks the fit used (every 1-yard hash tick it found). If the 10s are off by a line, name a line and fit again.

3Analyze the play again

Scrub the video above and press here to set each end, or type the times. Start the window at least half a second before the snap (the snap is found from the offensive line firing) and run it up to 5 s after it: the post-snap read is taken between 2 and 5 s after the snap. It does not have to include the frame you calibrated: the calibration is carried along the clip to the window's first frame and refit there.
