1Pick a still pre-snap frame

The frame the field is calibrated on. The suggested one is the last still frame before the play moves. Motion is the bar under each frame.

2Name the yard lines

Pick any line you can read a number on — the one through a painted 10, 20, 30... is easiest. The others follow 5 yards apart; a line the spacing says is not a yard line (the edge of end-zone paint, an overlay) is marked ✕ and left out.

Lines missed or wrong?

The yellow grid is labeled with painted yard numbers: they should sit on the painted numbers, and the grid on the painted yard lines, hashes and sidelines wherever players stand. Magenta circles are the landmarks the fit used (every 1-yard hash tick it found). If the numbers are off by a line, fix the painted number and fit again.

3Analyze the play

Scrub the video above and press here to set each end, or type the times. Start the window at least half a second before the snap (the snap is found from the offensive line firing) and run it through the coverage declaring, about 2.5 s after the snap. It does not have to include the frame you calibrated: the calibration is carried along the clip to the window's first frame and refit there.
